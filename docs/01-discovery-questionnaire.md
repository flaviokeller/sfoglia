# Discovery questionnaire

Fill this in **with** the client, not by sending it to them. Half of these
questions get a shrug in writing and a clear answer in conversation.

The awkward section at the end is the important one. These projects go wrong
through unspoken assumptions, not through technical problems — and precisely
because the relationship is informal, nobody raises them unprompted.

---

## 1. The basics

- What is the business, in one sentence?
- Who is the website for? (new customers / existing ones / referrers / suppliers)
- What should a visitor *do*? (call, book, come by, fill in the form, just trust you)
- Is there an existing site? What specifically is wrong with it?
- Are there competitor or reference sites they like? What do they like about them?

## 2. Scope

- Which pages are genuinely needed? (start small — pages can be added)
- Languages: which, and who writes the translations?
- Is there a booking system, newsletter, shop or online payment? **This changes
  everything about the project — flag it immediately.**
- Any embeds? (maps, video, social feeds, review widgets, chat)
  → each one is a third-party data transfer and makes the privacy policy longer

## 3. Content — usually the real bottleneck

- Who writes the text? By when?
- Are there usable photos, or is a photo shoot needed?
- Is there a logo? In what format? (a JPG of a logo is not a logo — ask for SVG,
  AI or EPS)
- Are there brand colours or a corporate design to follow?
- Who supplies the legal text — Impressum details, UID number?

> **The single most common reason these projects stall is missing content, not
> missing code.** Agree a date for text and photos, in writing.

## 4. Technical facts to establish

- **Who owns the domain?** Which registrar? Who has the login?
- Is there email on that domain? With whom?
  → this decides whether we can point nameservers at Netlify (we usually should
  not) or must add individual DNS records (we usually should)
- Where is the current site hosted, and can it be switched off?
- Does the client have a GitHub account, or are they willing to create one?
  → required to edit content themselves; takes three minutes, but some people
  will refuse, and it is much better to know now

## 5. Editing after launch

- How often do they realistically expect to change something?
  (be sceptical of "often" — most small sites are edited once or twice a year)
- Who does the editing? One person or several?
- What exactly do they want to change themselves: opening hours? prices? team? news?

## 6. Timing

- Is there a fixed date? What is driving it? (a real opening date is a
  constraint; "as soon as possible" is not)
- Who signs off, and is that person available?

---

## 7. The awkward questions — do not skip these

Ask them in a friendly, matter-of-fact way. They protect the friendship far more
than avoiding them does.

**Money and scope**
- Is this paid work, a favour, or something in between? Say the number out loud.
- What is explicitly **not** included? (content writing, photography, SEO
  campaigns, ongoing changes)
- How many rounds of revisions are included before it becomes new work?

**Running costs, and who pays**
- The domain costs money every year. Who pays and who renews it?
- Hosting is free at this scale — but that is a favour with a limit, not a
  contract. Say so.
- What happens if the site suddenly gets a lot of traffic?

**After launch**
- Who do they contact when something breaks? In what timeframe?
- Is there ongoing maintenance, or is this finished on launch day?
- Who updates dependencies in two years?

**Exit — the one everyone avoids**
- If they want someone else to take over, what happens? *(Answer: the content is
  in the repo, the site is static, and the whole thing moves to their own
  account in about fifteen minutes. Say that — it is reassuring and it is true.)*
- If you are unavailable — busy, ill, moved on — what is their fallback?
- Who has the passwords and where are they written down?
